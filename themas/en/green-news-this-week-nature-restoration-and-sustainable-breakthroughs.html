Green News This Week: Nature Restoration and Sustainable Breakthroughs

September 21, 2026 · BrightNews

This week on BrightNews is dedicated to the restoration and protection of nature, from birds and butterflies to elephants and foxes. In England, a former landfill has been transformed into the UK's most important breeding ground for great crested grebes, hosting 64% of all British breeding pairs. American states are working to help monarch butterflies during their journey of more than 3,200 kilometres to their wintering grounds in Mexico. In Nottinghamshire, a hit-and-run fox named Chance is recovering thanks to hydrotherapy in a dog swimming pool, and in East Africa, rangers from the Sheldrick Wildlife Trust are rescuing a baby elephant trapped in a water trough.

There is also much good happening in the field of sustainable innovation and urban greening. Toronto is developing a new neighbourhood with Ookwemin Minising featuring Canada's longest car-free street, where green space serves as climate infrastructure. French startup EverDye dyes textiles using static electricity instead of water and chemicals, significantly reducing energy and water consumption. In Florida, gene editing is set to save orange trees from the devastating citrus greening disease. And researchers from the University of Exeter and the Earthshot Prize have mapped 51 environmental solutions that, with the right support, could tip the planet towards positive change.

Together, these stories show that nature restoration and green innovation go hand in hand, across the globe.
