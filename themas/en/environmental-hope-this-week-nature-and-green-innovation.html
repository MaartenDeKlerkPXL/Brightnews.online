Environmental Hope This Week: Nature And Green Innovation

September 14, 2026 · BrightNews

This week is filled with stories showing how people worldwide are giving nature a second chance. From a former landfill in England that is now a breeding ground for nearly two-thirds of all British slavonian grebes, to a mountain spring in Idaho where cougars, elk and bears take turns at the watering hole.

Major players are also taking steps: Colombia protected 42% of its Amazon region against mining, while Ford has had a living roof of 454,000 square feet on its factory in Dearborn since 2002, where lapwings nest above the pickup truck production line.

In Egypt, farmers are experimenting with solar panels above their fields, which not only generate electricity but also protect crops from heat and evaporation. In Pennsylvania, an entomologist shows that a backyard with native plants can be just as valuable as a national park, with more than 1,380 moth species on his property.

There is also a look ahead: PBS Nature highlights how engineering can help save the planet, and to commemorate 25 years since 9/11, the organization 9/11 Day plants thousands of trees as part of a million good deeds. Together, these stories show that restoration, protection and innovation are taking place at all scales, from backyards to entire rainforests.
