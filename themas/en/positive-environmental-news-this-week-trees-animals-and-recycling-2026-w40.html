Positive environmental news this week: trees, animals and recycling

September 28, 2026 · BrightNews

This week is filled with hopeful news about nature restoration and animal welfare from around the world. In Ethiopia, citizens are planting trees en masse through the Green Legacy Initiative to restore lost forests, while researchers in Ivory Coast have rediscovered a rare bird species that had been missing for thirteen years. Nature is also full of surprises in Canada: the world's largest beaver dam was only discovered in 2007 through satellite imagery, hidden in a national park as large as Switzerland.

Animal rescuers also made headlines this week. At a wildlife sanctuary in Nairobi, an orphaned rhinoceros and an orphaned zebra formed a special friendship, while in India fishermen rescued a drowning elephant calf from a swollen river. In Georgia, an injured sea turtle received a custom-made 3D-printed harness to learn to swim again.

Finally, people are showing that waste doesn't have to be the end of the line: South Korea now recycles almost all of its food waste thanks to smart policy, and a group of teenagers from California set a world record by recycling 22,000 sports balls in a single day. Together, these stories demonstrate that dedication, innovation and cooperation lead to concrete results for people, animals and the environment all over the world.
